Lesson 1

Ask to Understand, Not Just to Answer

How you word a prompt shapes what you learn. A vague request gets a quick answer. A well-built one gets an explanation you can use, and a way to check that you understood it.

Four habits of a learning prompt

  • Give context. Say who you are and where you’re stuck.
  • Ask for an explanation. Request steps or an example, not only a definition.
  • Ask to be checked. Invite a practice question or quiz.
  • Set a boundary. Ask for hints so you still do the thinking.

Your task

You’re in an intro statistics course and standard deviation isn’t clicking. In the chat, write a prompt that helps you learn it, not just copy a definition. Revise and resend until the feedback shows all four habits.

Try: “I’m a first-year student new to statistics. Explain standard deviation step by step with a small example, then quiz me with one question. Give me hints instead of the full answer.”
